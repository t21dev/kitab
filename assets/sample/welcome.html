Kitab Reader · a short guide

Welcome to Kitab 📚

A reader for your PDFs and books that opens fast, stays out of the way, and keeps your files on your computer.

Open something

Drop a file onto the window, press Ctrl+O, or use Open file… on Home. Each document gets its own tab, and Kitab remembers the page you were on, so closing a book never loses your place. Home lists what you read lately, with how far through each one you are.

Getting around

Find your way 🧭

The sidebar holds the book's contents, your bookmarks, the pages and the marks you've made. Click any entry to jump there; Alt+← takes you back to where you were. The dock at the bottom shows the page number: drag its slider to move through the book, or click the number and type a page.

Search

Ctrl+F finds words in the open document, and F3 steps through the matches. The search box on Home looks through every document you have opened, and a result opens at the page it found.

Making notes

Mark it up ✍️

Press Ctrl+Shift+D for the markup tools: a pen, a highlighter, lines, arrows, boxes, text and sticky notes. Select some text and press H to highlight it. In a PDF, Ctrl+S saves your marks into the file as ordinary annotations, so any other reader shows them too.

🔒
About covers. The Cover tool draws a solid box over something, on screen and in print. The words underneath are still in the file, so don't send a covered PDF to hide a secret.

Passwords, signatures and pages

The lock in the title bar puts a password on a copy of a PDF, or takes one off. Sign… in the ⋯ menu signs a PDF with your certificate, and Kitab checks the signatures already in a file when it opens. Page tools… deletes, moves and turns pages, compresses a file, and adds page numbers or a watermark. Each of these saves a new file and leaves the original alone.

Make it yours

Read your way 🌙

Kitab comes with several themes, from Paper to Ink; Ctrl+Shift+L moves to the next one, and Settings (Ctrl+,) holds the rest: the accent colour, how dense the interface is, where the tabs and dock sit, and the typeface and margins for books.

F11 is focus mode: the tabs, sidebar and dock step aside and the page has the screen. The small buttons in the bottom-right corner bring back the contents or end focus mode. F5 presents a document full screen, one page at a time.

Yours, and only yours

Kitab sends nothing about you or your files anywhere. The only time it goes online is a quick check for a new version, which you can turn off in Settings › About. Your library, reading positions and marks stay in a small database on this computer.
