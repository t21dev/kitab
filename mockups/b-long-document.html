Kitab Reader · in development

Read beautifully. Work efficiently.

Kitab is a reader for PDF, EPUB, Markdown and comics. It is native and drawn on the GPU, it aims to open as fast as SumatraPDF, and it keeps your files on your computer. This page is laid out the way Kitab lays out a book: the contents on the left, your place at the bottom.

Speed is a test, not a claim

SumatraPDF is the bar for a light reader on Windows. Kitab's promise is to start and show the first page as fast as Sumatra on the same machine, or faster, and to stay smooth with many tabs open and while you resize the window.

No results yet. The first comparison ships with the first release, with the machine and the Sumatra version it ran on.

A benchmark starts both apps the same way and watches their windows until the page is drawn and stable. Each scenario runs fifteen times. A change that makes Kitab slower fails the build until it is fixed.

Open as many documents as you like in tabs. Close Kitab and open it again: every tab comes back on the same page, at the same zoom. Home shows what you were reading and where you stopped.

PDF pages keep their own size, so a landscape chart sits between portrait pages instead of being squeezed. Books reflow in Literata or Atkinson Hyperlegible. Markdown renders like GitHub, with Mermaid diagrams, highlighted code and math, and reloads when you save the file.

Press Ctrl F in any of them. Find works the same way everywhere.

Fill it, sign it, print it

Fill PDF forms, with totals and dates worked out the way the form expects, and no document scripts ever run. Open password-protected files of every standard kind. Check whether a signature is valid and whether the file changed after signing, or sign it yourself with a certificate that never leaves your computer.

Merge, split, compress, watermark and redact without uploading anything. Print with each page at its own orientation.

Your files stay where they are

There is no telemetry and no account. Kitab makes no network request until you turn something on, like the update check, and each setting says so where you turn it on. Your library, highlights and settings live in one folder. Delete it and they are gone.

Where it stands

Kitab is in its first phase: the foundations, and a test of whether the interface framework can start as fast as Sumatra. Every phase after that ends with its tests passing and a release. The plan is public, and so is every line of code.
